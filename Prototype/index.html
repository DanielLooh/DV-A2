Banking is going digital. Is everyone coming along?

Australians now do almost all their banking on phones and computers. Between 2017 and 2026 the country lost close to half its bank branches. This prototype puts two questions on the map: who is ready for digital banking, and where has face-to-face banking gone?

Digital readiness is highest in the ACT and lowest in Tasmania

The Australian Digital Inclusion Index scores each state from 0 to 100 on whether people can get online, afford it and have the skills to use it. The ACT scores 79.5. Tasmania scores 69.4, and South Australia is next lowest at 71.3.

Hover over a state to see its three sub-scores.

Every state has lost at least 29% of its branches

Nationally the number of bank branches fell from 5,694 in June 2017 to 3,108 in June 2026, a drop of 45%. Western Australia lost the most (52.5%). The Northern Territory lost the least (29.0%), though it started with only 62 branches.

Darker means a bigger share of branches closed.

Branches cluster on the coast, and the clusters are shrinking

Each circle shows the branches within a quarter-degree square, roughly 25 kilometres across. Switch between 2017 and 2026 to see the large city clusters thin out. Inland, many towns have few branches to lose.

Circle area is proportional to the number of branches.
